# Moderator script

Read the lines in quotes out loud, word for word. Everything else is for you.
Keep your voice warm and calm. Never explain the app. Never say "just click...".

## Before you start

- Practice mode badge is visible. Fresh data folder. CV on the desktop. Stopwatch ready.
- Task cards (the quoted prompts below) printed or open on a second screen. For P3, use `script.ar.md`.

## Intro and warm-up (5 minutes)

**Say:**

"Hi, and thank you for coming. I'm going to tell you a few things before we start, and I'll read them so I don't forget any.

Today we're trying out an app called LinKit All. It finds jobs, adjusts your CV and fills in applications for you. Everything stays on this computer.

We're testing the app, not you. You can't do anything wrong. If something is confusing, that's the app's fault, and that is exactly what I want to find out. So please be honest, even if it feels rude.

I'll ask you to think out loud. That means saying what you're looking at, what you're trying to do, and what you expect to happen. Say it even if it sounds silly: 'I'm looking for a button that does X', 'I don't know what this word means'. There are no wrong thoughts.

I can't help you during the tasks, because I want to see what it's like when you're on your own. If I stay quiet, that's why. You can't break anything: it's in practice mode, and nothing is sent to any company.

You can stop at any time, for any reason, and you don't have to say why.

Here is a short note about what happens with your information. Please read it, and ask me anything."

**Hand over the consent note.** Wait. Then ask: "Are you happy for us to start? And is it OK to record the screen and your voice? It's fine to say no."

Note the answers in the notes sheet.

**Think-aloud practice (1 minute).** Say: "Let's practise once. Please open the Start menu and tell me what you'd do to find the Calculator, and say what you're thinking as you go." Let them talk. Praise: "That's exactly it."

Then: "Great. I'll start the clock for each task when you begin. It's not a test: the times just help me see where the app is slow."

## Neutral probes (use in this order, only when needed)

Wait at least 20 seconds of silence first. Write down every probe you use (help level 1).

1. "What are you thinking now?"
2. "What were you expecting to see?"
3. "What would you do if I weren't here?"
4. "What are you looking for?"
5. "Is there anything on the screen that you don't understand?"

Direct hints are help level 2, for example "Have you looked at the top menu?". Use one only after 90 seconds of being stuck, and mark the task "partial" at best.
Doing it for them is help level 3: the task is "no".

If they ask "Is this right?": "What do you think?". If they ask "What does this word mean?": "What do you think it means?" (and note the word).

## Task 1: from install to the first search

**Success:** the participant uploads a CV, connects the AI, checks the details, picks the kind of jobs and starts the first search **without help**, in 8 minutes or less. The goal is 3 minutes or less from choosing the CV to the search starting, and 5 decisions or fewer.

**Time limit:** 8 minutes. Clock starts when they start working; stop it when the first search starts (Home shows live progress).

**Before:** the app is open at the welcome screen. The AI tool is already installed and signed in on this computer.

**Say:** "Imagine you've just installed this app because you want to find a new job. Your CV is the file called *[CV name]* on the desktop. Please get the app to start looking for jobs for you. Remember to think out loud."

**Observe:**
- Do they find the CV upload (drop or Choose a file)? Do they understand which formats work?
- Do they notice the language switch (English / العربية)?
- On "Connect your AI": do they understand what it is? Do they know which card to pick? Does the automatic test make sense? Do they wait or click away?
- On "Check what we found": do they read the values? Do they notice the "From your CV" source badges and the "check this" warnings? Do they fix a wrong value? Do they know what "Never changed in your CV" means?
- The sensitive questions (work authorization, demographic, salary): do they understand "Ask me"? Do they feel pressure to answer?
- On "What jobs?": do they understand the job titles, level, and Where? Do they see that this is practice mode?
- Count the decisions. Count hesitations over 10 seconds. Note anything they say about LibreOffice.
- Does the step counter ("Step 2 of 4") and Back work for them?

**Mark:** yes / partial / no, time, decisions, errors, quotes, severity.

**Reset after:** let the search run in the background, or stop it. Switch to the prepared data folder (see the README) before Task 2.

## Task 2: why was a job filtered?

**Success:** the participant finds the filtered job and says the correct reason in their own words (for example "the title has 'intern'"), in 4 minutes, without help.

**Time limit:** 4 minutes.

**Before:** the prepared data folder is open on Home. Pick the one filtered job the participant should look for. Write its title and company on a card.

**Say:** "Here is a job you were hoping to see: *[job title]* at *[company]*. The app didn't show it in your good matches. Please find out why."

**Observe:**
- Where do they go first: Home funnel numbers, Jobs, a filter, a search box?
- Do they find the reason chip on the job ("Filtered: ...")? Do they find "Show me why" in the job detail?
- Do they notice that the funnel numbers on Home are links?
- Do they understand "Filtered", "Strong / Possible / Weak match" and "Stale posting"?
- Can they say what they would change if they disagree? Do they find the setting?

**Mark:** yes / partial / no, time, errors, quotes, severity.

## Task 3: one answer for four applications

**Success:** the participant finds the waiting applications, answers the shared question **once**, and all four applications are retried, in 5 minutes, without help.

**Time limit:** 5 minutes.

**Before:** four applications are parked with the same question (for example "What is your notice period?"). A "Needs you" badge shows in the navigation. Do not mention the badge.

**Say:** "The app has told you that some of your applications are stuck. Please get them moving again."

**Observe:**
- Do they notice the "Needs you" badge and open it? Which tab do they choose: "Approve jobs" or "Finish applications"?
- Do they understand that four applications are grouped under one question?
- Do they answer once, or open each application one by one?
- Do they see "Use this for similar questions"? Do they understand it? Do they leave it on?
- Do they understand what happens after they answer? Do they see the four retries?
- Do they understand each card: what happened, why, and the one main button?
- Is the empty state ("Nothing needs you...") clear?
- If a sensitive question (salary, work authorization) appears, do they trust that nothing is guessed?

**Mark:** yes / partial / no, time, errors, quotes, severity.

## Task 4: turn on real applications (stop at the confirm)

**Success:** the participant finds the "Ready to apply for real?" task, goes through the checklist, reaches the confirm dialog ("Submit real applications from now on?"), says in their own words what it would do, and then **cancels**. The participant must **not** confirm. 5 minutes, without help.

**Time limit:** 5 minutes.

**Before:** the prepared data folder has a finished practice run with at least one filled form. The header still says "Practice mode". Confirm that no live submission is possible: nothing is queued that you are not happy to leave unsent.

**Say:** "You've seen the app practise on a few jobs and you're happy with how it fills the forms. Now you'd like it to apply for real. Please show me how you'd do that. **When you get to the last confirmation, please stop and tell me what you think will happen, but don't press the confirm button.** I'll tell you when we're done."

If they move to press Confirm, say gently: "Please stop there and tell me what you expect to happen." If they press it anyway, cancel or switch the data folder immediately, mark the issue as severity 4, and note it.

**Observe:**
- Do they find the Home task "Ready to apply for real?" Do they look in Settings first?
- Do they look at the screenshots of the filled forms and the CV diff? Do they understand the red-line diff?
- Do they understand the checklist: CV approved, sensitive answers set or on "Ask me", daily caps (Pace), "Confirm each submission" on?
- Do they understand the difference between "Practice mode" and "Applying for real"?
- Do they understand the confirm dialog? Are they afraid, or surprised? What do they think "Submit real applications from now on?" means?
- Do they understand that "Submit without asking" is a separate setting? Do they find it by accident?
- Do they say they would feel safe turning this on?

**Mark:** yes / partial / no, time, errors, quotes, severity.

**Reset after:** cancel the dialog. Confirm the badge still says "Practice mode".

## SUS and debrief (10 minutes)

**Say:** "Thank you, that was really useful. I have a short questionnaire with ten statements. Please answer quickly, with your first feeling. There are no right answers."

Hand over `sus.md` (or `sus.ar.md`). Wait. Collect it. Do not discuss the answers yet.

**Debrief questions** (ask in this order; follow up with "Tell me more"):

1. "How was that, overall, in your own words?"
2. "What was the easiest part? What was the hardest part?"
3. "Was there a moment you felt lost? Where?"
4. "Were there any words in the app you didn't understand?"
5. "Did you feel you were in control of what the app does? Was there a moment you weren't sure what it would do?"
6. "How did you feel about the app using your CV and the AI? Was anything about your privacy unclear?"
7. "How do you feel about letting the app apply for you for real? What would make you trust it more?"
8. *(Arabic-UI participant)* "Did anything in Arabic sound strange or look wrong, for example the text direction, the buttons or the numbers?"
9. *(Developers)* "Which technical term would you change? Was there anything you wanted that you couldn't find?"
10. "If you could change one thing, what would it be?"
11. "Would you use this app for your real job search? Why, or why not?"

Close: "Thank you so much. In about a week, I'll ask you to spend 15 minutes with the app again. It'll be a short task like today's. I won't tell you what it'll be about, and please don't prepare. Is next *[day]* OK?"

Delete the data folder and CV copy afterwards (see the README).

## Task 5: the return after 7 days (separate 15-minute session)

**Success:** the participant opens the app, finds what is waiting, approves the jobs they like and starts applying (in practice mode), in 5 minutes, **without help or a reminder** of how the app works. The target is 4 of 5 participants.

**Time limit:** 5 minutes (the rest of the 15 minutes is a short chat).

**Before:** the participant's own data was deleted after the first session (the consent note promises this), so restore the prepared folder, with some "Possible" matches waiting for approval and some "Strong" ones queued. Say nothing about what changed. Do not look at the app together first. Make sure the badge says "Practice mode".

**Say:** "Welcome back. Last week you tried LinKit All. Imagine you left it for a week and now you've come back. Please do whatever you'd normally do to get your applications going. Please think out loud, as before."

**Observe:**
- Do they remember where to start? What do they click first? Does Home's main button ("Review N possible matches" or "Apply to N jobs (practice)") guide them?
- Do they approve jobs? Do they use the shortcut keys or bulk approve? Do they notice the 10-second Undo?
- Do they start applying? Do they understand that this is practice?
- What do they remember from last time? What have they forgotten? Which words do they still not know?
- Do they ask for help? How many times?

**Mark:** yes / partial / no, time, errors, quotes, severity. Then ask debrief questions 1, 3, 10 and "What did you remember from last week?".

**Stop:** Practice mode must stay on. Thank them. Delete everything.
